Every year thousands of people walk to the top of Kilimanjaro without any technical climbing skills. The route you choose, and especially the number of days, has the biggest effect on whether you reach the summit.

## Why more days help

The main challenge is altitude. Routes that climb high during the day and sleep lower at night, and that add an extra day on the way up, give your body time to adjust. Seven or eight days has a much better summit rate than five or six.

## The main routes

- **Machame (6–7 days):** the most popular route, with varied scenery and good acclimatisation. Busy in peak season.
- **Lemosho (7–8 days):** starts in the remote west, quieter in the first days and very scenic. Our choice for the best chance of success.
- **Rongai (6–7 days):** approaches from the north, near the Kenyan border. Drier and quieter, good in the rainy months.
- **Marangu (5–6 days):** the only route with huts instead of tents, but a shorter climb profile and a lower success rate.

## When to climb

The driest months are January to early March and June to October. It is possible to climb at other times, but expect rain in the forest and snow higher up.
