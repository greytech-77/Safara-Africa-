Around 1.5 million wildebeest, with hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle, move in a loop through the Serengeti and Kenya's Maasai Mara every year, following the rain and fresh grass. There is no single "migration season": the herds are somewhere in the ecosystem all year. What changes is where, and what they are doing.

## December to March: the calving season

The herds spread across the short-grass plains of the southern Serengeti and Ndutu. Most calves are born in a few weeks around February, which draws lion, cheetah and hyena. It is one of the best times for predator action.

## April to June: on the move

The long rains turn the plains green and the herds begin to move north and west through the central Serengeti and the Western Corridor, where they cross the Grumeti River. Some camps close in April and May, and prices are at their lowest.

## July to October: the river crossings

The herds reach the northern Serengeti and the Mara River. The crossings, with crocodiles waiting, are the most famous images of the migration. This is peak season, so book early.

## November: heading south

With the short rains, the herds return south towards the calving plains, and the cycle starts again.

## Choosing your camp

Timing shifts with the rain each year. We follow reports from guides in the field and place you in camps, often mobile tented camps, close to where the herds are expected.
